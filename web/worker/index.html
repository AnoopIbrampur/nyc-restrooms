Picking up an order? You can use the restroom.

A guide for NYC app delivery workers (food, grocery and other goods).

Your right, in short

  • When you pick up an order at a restaurant, grocery or any other business in NYC, it must let you use a restroom on the premises.
  • If it has no customer restroom, its staff restroom counts.
  • A business may say no only if reaching the restroom means walking through the kitchen, food-prep, storage or dish area, or if there's an obvious health or safety risk.
  • If you're wrongly refused, you may be owed $200 each time.

    What the law says

    The rule (Admin Code §20-1527, since Jan 26, 2026)
    "A food service establishment or other business … that sells or provides goods delivered by a contracted delivery worker shall provide access to a toilet facility located on such premises to a contracted delivery worker that picks up such goods…"

    "Contracted delivery worker" means someone retained by a delivery app or service to deliver goods. The pickup includes selecting, assembling or packing goods in the store, so it covers grocery shoppers too. A "toilet facility" is the restroom for customers, or the staff restroom if there's no customer restroom.

    When a business can say no
    • Reaching the restroom would mean walking through a kitchen, food preparation or storage area, or utensil-washing area.
    • Access would create an obvious health or safety risk to you or the business.
    • Any exception DCWP creates by rule. None found as of the date below.
    What you can get, and the penalties

    You may be owed $200 per refusal, plus anything needed to make you whole (§20-1508). The business can face civil penalties of $500 for a first violation, up to $750 for a second and up to $1,000 for each later one within two years (§20-1509).

    Older rule for app–restaurant contracts

    Since Jan 24, 2022, delivery apps' contracts with restaurants must also require restroom access for food delivery workers, with the same kitchen and health-or-safety exceptions (Local Law 117 of 2021).

    Is the law being challenged?

    The 2025 delivery-worker laws are in effect. Instacart's lawsuit against the grocery-delivery rules is on appeal (2d Cir., No. 26-172). The courts have refused to pause the laws while it continues.

    Reports from workers

    No reports are published yet.

    How reports will work: you tap let in, refused, told the restroom is through the kitchen or no restroom. There's no name, no text and no account. A business is listed only after at least 3 different workers report being refused there within 6 months, and refusals are at least half of its reports. Businesses can dispute a listing. "Through the kitchen" is a legal exception and is never counted as a refusal. A listing means workers reported being refused here, not that the business broke the law.
