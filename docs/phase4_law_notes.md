# Delivery-worker restroom access: what NYC law says

**Last checked: 2026-11-27.** Summarized from the enacted local-law texts and DCWP's pages. Not legal advice. The worker page's legal copy comes only from this file.

## The rule (since January 26, 2026)
**Admin Code §20-1527**, added by **Local Law 123 of 2025**:

> "A food service establishment or other business with a premises located in the city that sells or provides goods delivered by a contracted delivery worker shall provide access to a toilet facility located on such premises to a contracted delivery worker that picks up such goods…"

- **Who must allow it:** any business in NYC with premises where a contracted delivery worker picks up goods. That covers restaurants and also groceries, convenience stores, pharmacies and retail. **The right attaches to the pickup**, not to a list of places.
- **Who has the right:** "contracted delivery workers", meaning people retained by a delivery service (app) to deliver goods, food delivery workers included. Drivers of vehicles that must be registered with the federal motor carrier agency are excluded (§20-1501).
- **What counts as a toilet facility:** a room with a toilet or urinal and a sink that is "a dedicated facility for its patrons, or … for its employees to the extent such establishment or business does not have a dedicated facility for its patrons" (§20-1501). **So a staff restroom counts when there's no customer restroom.**
- **The pickup includes** "selecting, assembling, or packing such goods on such premises" (relevant for grocery shoppers).

## Exceptions (§20-1527(b))
A business doesn't have to provide access where:
1. reaching it "would require a contracted delivery worker to walk through a kitchen, food preparation or storage area, or utensil washing area of a food service establishment";
2. it "would create an obvious health or safety risk" to the worker or the business; or
3. DCWP has created an exception by rule. **None found as of 2026-11-27.**

## Remedies
- **To the worker:** $200 per worker per violation of §20-1527 (§20-1508(a)(3)(f), "per worker and per instance"), plus making the worker whole.
- **Civil penalty:** "any person" violating the chapter is liable for **$500** for a first violation, up to **$750** for a second and up to **$1,000** for each one after that within 2 years, per worker and per instance (§20-1509).
- **Complaints:** DCWP online form (topic "delivery worker"), or call 311 and ask for "Delivery Worker". DCWP says it won't share your identity without consent and never asks about immigration status. Retaliation is illegal (§20-1504).

## Earlier law (still in force for app–restaurant contracts)
**Local Law 117 of 2021**, Admin Code §20-563.6(b): agreements between third-party food delivery apps and restaurants, executed on or after the law's effective date (DCWP: January 24, 2022), must require that a toilet facility be available to food delivery workers picking up orders. The exceptions are the same kitchen and obvious health-or-safety ones, plus any set by rule.

## Status
Local Laws 123 and 124 of 2025 are in effect. Instacart's challenge to the grocery-delivery laws is on appeal (2d Cir., No. 26-172). The district court denied a preliminary injunction in January 2026, and the Second Circuit refused to pause the laws pending appeal on May 21, 2026.
